Flowers do not respect a wedding date. Our job is to know which blooms will be at their best on the
day you have chosen, and to be honest with you when the one you love is not.

## The Kenyan growing calendar, in practice

Kenya grows flowers year round, but not the same flowers. Long rains run roughly from March into
May, short rains from late October into December, and the coolest months are June, July and August.
Each shift changes stem length, colour depth and how long a bloom holds.

- **January to February.** Warm, bright and generous. Roses are at their strongest, and this is the
  easiest window of the year for almost anything you want.
- **March to May.** Rain season. Blooms are softer and more delicate. We build in more foliage,
  because greenery carries a design through weather that can knock cut flowers back.
- **June to August.** Cool and dry. This is the season for roses, carnations, gypsophila and the
  deeper colours. Delphinium and stock behave well, and candles work beautifully early in the evening.
- **September to October.** Our favourite window. Warm days, cool nights, and the widest choice of
  the year. Book early, because everybody else thinks the same.
- **November to December.** Short rains and a busy calendar. Hydrangea, roses and imported blooms
  carry the month, and demand pushes prices up. Secure your date first, then your flowers.

## Local or imported

Kenyan roses are world class. If a rose works in your palette, use it, and you will pay local
prices for something that arrives fresher than anything that has crossed a border.

There is a real case for imports, though. Peonies, ranunculus and certain garden roses are not
grown here commercially, and specific colours are simply not available locally in some months. The
freight is real, and so is the risk of transit damage, so we plan a local substitution for every
imported variety before you commit to it.

## Condition, and why it decides everything

Half of what a florist is paid for happens before the arrangement exists. Stems get cut at an angle,
stripped, hydrated in clean water with the correct conditioning solution, and held at the right
temperature. Bouquets built on the morning from flowers conditioned properly on Thursday will still
look right at the end of a long day.

That is why we build bridal work last, not first, and why we arrive at the venue with conditioned
stock rather than shopping for it that morning.

## Practical numbers

- **Bridal bouquet.** Around 25 to 40 stems for a generous hand tied bouquet. More stems is not
  always better. Structure matters more.
- **Centrepieces.** One arrangement per four to six guests works for round tables. For long tables,
  plan a runner in sections so guests can still pass a plate.
- **Installations.** Budget on stem count, not on square metres. A dense arch takes three to four
  times the flowers of a sparse one on the same frame.
- **Boutonnieres.** One per person who will be photographed from the chest up, plus two spares.
  Always two spares.

## The one thing to book first

Secure the date with your decorator before you fall in love with a specific flower. Dates come
first, flowers follow, and a good florist will tell you honestly when an idea needs to shift by
a fortnight to look the way you pictured it.
