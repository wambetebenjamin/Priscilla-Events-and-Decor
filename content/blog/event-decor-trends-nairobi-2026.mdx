Every January we sit down with the year's enquiries and look for the patterns. Here is what
Nairobi is asking us for in 2026, and our honest view on which of these will still look good in
five years.

## 1. Quiet luxury has replaced the statement wall

For three years the request was a floral wall. This year, clients are asking us to spend the same
money on things guests actually touch: heavier linen, better glassware, real candles, and flowers
that are arranged rather than stacked.

The result is a room that photographs beautifully but reads even better in person, which is the
opposite of how a feature wall works. A wall performs for the camera. A table performs for the
people sitting at it.

> Guests forget the ceiling. They remember their own table.

## 2. Full ceiling draping, done properly

Draping has moved from the reception backdrop to the ceiling itself. We dressed four venues this
way in the last quarter alone, and the reason is simple: a draped ceiling changes the acoustics
and the light at the same time as it changes the look.

Done badly it is a fire and rigging risk. Done properly it needs a venue survey, a load in plan
and a crew that understands what the structure above the ceiling can carry. Ask any decorator how
they intend to fix the fabric, and where, before you pay a deposit.

## 3. Garden weddings, and a real plan for the weather

Karen, Limuru, Nanyuki and the ridge venues above Ngong are busier than we have ever seen them.
The look has settled into something calmer: sand and terracotta, olive greenery, and one strong
floral moment at the aisle rather than flowers everywhere.

The part nobody talks about is rain. Every garden wedding we style now carries a wet weather plan:
a marquee hold, a covered aisle route, and a decision time written into the run of show so the
couple is not asked to make a hard call at the last minute.

## 4. Lighting as a line item, not an afterthought

Two years ago, maybe one client in five asked about lighting. Now it is closer to four in five, and
corporate clients ask about it first.

That is a good change. Lighting is what carries a room from the ceremony into the speeches and then
into the dancing, and it can be the difference between a hall that feels borrowed and a room that
feels built for the evening.

## 5. Shorter guest lists, higher spend per guest

Guest counts are down and per guest budgets are up. A couple who once invited three hundred and
fifty people is now inviting two hundred and twenty, and putting the difference into flowers,
linen and food.

We like this. Fewer tables means each one can be properly dressed, and a room that is correctly
sized for its guest list always looks better than a room that is half empty.

## 6. What we think will last

- **Local flowers.** Kenyan growers produce roses, hydrangeas and foliage that are as good as
  anything imported, and they arrive fresher and cheaper. This is not a trend.
- **Warm light over white light.** Cold white uplighting flatters nothing and nobody.
- **Tables people can talk across.** Low arrangements and candle light beat tall centrepieces on
  every single brief.
- **One idea, done fully.** The events that date fastest are the ones that tried five ideas at once.

## What we are quietly retiring

Balloon arches at weddings. Tall centrepieces that block sight lines. Cold white uplighting.
And the floral wall, unless it is genuinely the best thing in the room.
