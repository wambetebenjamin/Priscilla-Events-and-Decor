The brief, as the couple gave it to us: a garden wedding in Karen, one hundred and eighty guests,
sand and terracotta, and the words "please do not make it look like a hotel".

## The space

A private residence with a broad lawn, a mature jacaranda at the far end and a house wall that
would end up behind the head table whether we liked it or not.

Our first decision was to make the jacaranda the ceremony. Everything else in the garden was
arranged to point at it.

## The build

| Element | What we did |
| --- | --- |
| Aisle | Sheer draping on a curved timber frame, flowers clustered at three points rather than along the whole run |
| Seating | Cross backed chairs in natural timber, no chair covers |
| Ceremony | A simple platform under the tree, with the existing branches left visible above it |
| Reception | A clear span marquee styled open on two sides, so the garden stayed part of the room |
| Tables | Long tables, sand linen runners, terracotta candle clusters, low arrangements |
| Lighting | Festoon through the tree canopy and warm uplighting on the house wall |

The flowers were seasonal and local: spray roses, stocks, olive foliage and eucalyptus, with
terracotta and sand tones carried through the linen and the candles. The only imported stems were
in the bridal bouquet.

## The weather call

Three days out, the forecast was for evening rain. We set a decision point at 2pm on the day, agreed
in writing with the couple a month in advance so nobody had to make a hard call in the moment.

At 2pm we kept the ceremony outdoors and dropped the marquee sides for the reception. The rain
arrived at 9:40pm, six minutes after the cake was cut. The dancing moved inside without a single
guest noticing anything had been decided at all.

## What worked

- **One strong idea.** The tree. Everything else stayed quiet.
- **No carpet.** Grass underfoot, and a timber platform only where the ceremony needed level ground.
- **Low flowers.** Guests talked across every table all night.
- **A real build window.** We had eight hours and we used seven and a half of them.

## What we would do differently

The receiving line ran alongside the draping, which meant the fabric took every hand print and every
glass of wine from the first forty minutes. Next time we would move the line clear of the fabric and
put a designated drinks station between the two.

## The result

A garden that still looked like a garden, a couple who stayed outside in it until the rain arrived,
and a photograph of the aisle that the couple now have framed in their hallway.
